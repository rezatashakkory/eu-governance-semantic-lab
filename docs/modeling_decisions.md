# Modeling decisions

Short decision records. Each one states the question, the choice, the reason, and the cost.

## D1. SKOS for the thesaurus, OWL for the legal acts

**Question.** Which standard should model which part of the project?

**Choice.** The 68 topics are a SKOS thesaurus. The legal acts and their relations are described with a small OWL ontology.

**Why.**
- Topics are *terms with meaning*: labels in several languages, definitions, broader and narrower links. SKOS was designed for exactly this, and EuroVoc itself is a SKOS thesaurus, so alignment is natural.
- Acts are *things with relations*: one act amends or repeals another. OWL gives these relations a precise meaning (inverse, asymmetric, sub-property) so a reasoner can check and extend them.
- Broader and narrower in SKOS do not mean "is a subclass of". *Right to erasure* is not a kind of *Data subject*. It is a concept placed under it for navigation. OWL subclassing would claim something false.

**Cost.** Two vocabularies to learn and explain. The bridge is `lab:hasTopic`, which points from an act to a `skos:Concept`.

## D2. No separate Topic class

**Question.** The first plan had a `Topic` class in the ontology. Keep it?

**Choice.** No. `lab:hasTopic` has the range `skos:Concept`. Topics are the concepts of the thesaurus.

**Why.** A `Topic` class would duplicate `skos:Concept` and force every concept to be typed twice. One source of truth is simpler.

## D3. `regulates` is a sub-property of `hasTopic`

**Choice.** `lab:regulates` marks the one main subject of an act. Every regulated subject is also a topic.

**Why.** It shows sub-property reasoning with a real benefit: the data says `regulates` once, and the reasoner adds `hasTopic`. It also explains why query 11 needs the path `lab:hasTopic|lab:regulates` when no reasoner is running. Test `test_reasoning_makes_has_topic_alone_enough` proves the difference.

## D4. The URI design

**Choice.** One base URI, `https://rezatashakkory.github.io/eu-governance-semantic-lab/`, with these patterns:

| Kind | Pattern | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ontology terms | `/def/Name` | `/def/LegalAct` |
| Thesaurus concepts | `/id/concept/slug` | `/id/concept/data-protection` |
| Concept scheme | `/id/scheme/slug` | `/id/scheme/eu-data-governance` |
| Legal acts | `/id/act/CELEX` | `/id/act/32016R0679` |

**Why.**
- Concept slugs are readable English words. They never change, even if a label is reworded, because the label is data and the URI is the name.
- Acts use the CELEX number as the local name. It is the identifier lawyers already use, so no new numbering scheme is invented.
- `/def/` (definitions of terms) and `/id/` (identified things) follows the common linked data convention of separating the vocabulary from the data.
- Our act URIs are *our description* of an act. The official identifier is kept as a separate value, `lab:hasELI`, and we do not declare `owl:sameAs` to it. That would merge our statements with everyone else's statements about the official resource.

**The limit of a static site.** GitHub Pages serves files. It cannot look at the `Accept` header and return Turtle to one client and HTML to another. This is called content negotiation, and it is the fourth linked data principle in practice. Here the URIs *name* things reliably, but they do not *dereference* to RDF by themselves. The honest options are:
1. Accept the limit and publish the files in `data/dist` (done).
2. Use a persistent redirect service such as w3id.org to point the URIs to files (needs a pull request to their repository).
3. Host on a server that can negotiate content. The Publications Office does this for ELI and EuroVoc.

The project does not claim more than option 1.

## D5. Turtle is the source of truth

**Choice.** Hand-edited Turtle files are the only source. JSON-LD and RDF/XML in `data/dist` are generated by `python -m semlab build`.

**Why.** Turtle is the most readable format and diffs well in git. A test (`test_committed_dist_files_match_the_turtle_sources`) fails if a generated file is out of date, so the formats can never drift.

## D6. Own labels, not copied EuroVoc

**Choice.** The thesaurus has its own labels, definitions and structure. Ten concepts take their French and German labels from EuroVoc, and 19 concepts link to EuroVoc with `skos:exactMatch`, `skos:closeMatch` or `skos:broadMatch`.

**Why.** This project covers a narrow slice (data and AI governance) with definitions written for non-experts. Mapping links give interoperability without claiming the project *is* EuroVoc.

**How the match type was chosen.**
- `exactMatch`: the two concepts mean the same thing (for example *Data protection*).
- `closeMatch`: very similar, but not safe to substitute everywhere (for example *AI system* and *artificial intelligence*, a system versus a field).
- `broadMatch`: the EuroVoc concept is broader than ours (for example *ICT risk management* and *risk management*).

The mapping is the author's judgment and should be reviewed by someone who knows EuroVoc well.

## D7. Translations are flagged, not hidden

**Choice.** A French or German label that does not come from EuroVoc carries `lab:pendingReview` on its concept. The app shows a visible chip, and `docs/translations_to_review.md` lists every one.

**Why.** It is honest and it makes review easy. A property on the concept is used because RDF cannot attach a note to a plain literal. The alternative, SKOS-XL labels, can carry status per label but is heavier than this project needs.

## D8. SHACL checks and OWL reasoning do different jobs

**Choice.** SHACL validates data quality. OWL reasoning derives facts and detects contradictions.

**Why.** OWL assumes the world is open: a missing label is not an error, it is unknown. SHACL assumes a closed view of the data and says "this must be present". We need both. See the table in `STUDY_GUIDE.md`.

## D9. Reasoning is a demonstration, not a dependency

**Choice.** All 15 queries and all SHACL shapes work on the asserted triples only. Reasoning runs on demand in the app and the CLI.

**Why.** Everything works with a plain SPARQL endpoint that has no reasoner. Reasoning shows what an ontology adds without making the project depend on it.

## D10. What was cut

- **Fuseki with Docker Compose.** Docker is not installed on the development machine, so the file could not be tested. An untested compose file would break the rule that everything must run. It is listed under future work.
- **Live Cellar queries in the app.** The Cellar module exists, has tests, and is used by `python -m semlab verify-acts`, but it is not a tab in the app. A live network call inside a demo adds a way for the demo to fail.
